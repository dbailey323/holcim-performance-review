Year end performance 2026

The dates have moved this year, and the performance window opens on Monday 19th October. This page is for managers, and it takes you through what happens and when, what a good review rests on, and how to handle the moments that are hardest to get right.

The performance window opens on Monday 19th October.

The four dates to know

Everything in this year's process works back from these four dates. The first three are yours to meet as a manager, and the last is the sign-off stage that follows.

What you need to do

These are the six steps for every manager, from gathering evidence through to sign-off in December.

  1. Now

    Ask your team to start their self-assessment

    Each colleague assesses themselves against their objectives and gives their own view of their rating in SuccessFactors. Ask your team to start thinking about this now, so that it is submitted by the time they sit down with you. Point them to the rating descriptions so that they are working from the same definitions as you.

  2. From 19th October

    Gather evidence for each person in your team

    Review each colleague's objectives against what was delivered across the full year. Look for evidence of how they worked as well as what they achieved, and ask others who worked closely with them where you have gaps.

  3. 19th October to 13th November

    Hold the performance conversation

    The colleague's self-assessment forms the basis of the conversation, so read it beforehand and form your own view from the evidence before you compare the two. Start with where you agree, then talk through where you differ and why. Share where you think they are, based on their objectives and their performance in the year, and be clear that this could change following calibration. Give each colleague proper time and a private space. Nothing you say should come as a surprise, so if it will, slow down and explain the evidence behind your view before you talk about anything else.

  4. By Friday 13th November

    Enter your proposed ratings in SuccessFactors

    Record a proposed rating for every colleague with a short, evidence-based rationale. This date is fixed, because calibration cannot start until every manager's ratings are in. Ratings also need to be in on time because they inform the annual pay review, which opens in January.

  5. 16th to 27th November

    Calibrate with your peers

    Calibration runs for the two weeks after ratings close. It is led by your People Business Partner, starting at senior level and then moving to the next layer of managers. It looks at every colleague and not only the highest and lowest performers, so come ready to explain each rating and to change your mind when the evidence supports it. Once a rating has been calibrated, enter the agreed outcome for that colleague in SuccessFactors before calibration closes on 27th November.

  6. During December

    Treat ratings as provisional until sign-off

    The CEO signs off the final ratings on Friday 18th December. Until that has happened a rating can still change, so please do not share it with your colleague as final. Confirmed ratings are shared with colleagues after the CEO has signed them off.

What a good review rests on

The dates tell you when things have to happen. These three ideas are what make the review worth having.

The what and the how

We look at what someone achieved and at how they achieved it. A strong result reached in a way that damaged safety, trust or teamwork is not a strong performance.

Evidence from the whole year

A rating should rest on examples from January to now, measured against the objectives that were agreed. It should not rest on the last six weeks or on a general impression.

A conversation without surprises

The year end conversation should bring together feedback the colleague has already heard. High challenge and high support both belong in the same conversation.

The five ratings

These are the five Holcim performance ratings and what each one means. Good Performance is the rating most colleagues receive, and it describes someone who meets and sometimes exceeds what their role and objectives ask of them.

Outstanding Performance

A colleague who sets ambitious targets and objectives and consistently goes beyond them. They are highly driven by results, have a strong growth mindset for the business and for themselves, and always look to contribute more. They challenge the status quo, bring others with them and help to build a high-performance culture. Others greatly value what they deliver and see them as a role model.

High Performance

A colleague who consistently achieves challenging targets and objectives. They have a continuous improvement mindset and raise their own performance over time. They are reliable and resilient, perform consistently on new projects and assignments, and face new challenges with confidence. They challenge the status quo, drive change and collaborate strongly, and their contribution is well appreciated by others.

Good Performance

A colleague who meets and sometimes exceeds the requirements of their role and their objectives. They are reliable, steady and competent, and they identify areas for improvement and act on them. They are able and willing to adapt to new situations, although the most demanding challenges may stretch them beyond their comfort zone. They bring good energy, drive and passion to the job and are well appreciated for their professionalism and competence.

Inconsistent Performance

A colleague who has not always delivered on their objectives this year, with performance and results that have varied. Their effort, energy or drive has not consistently been at the level the role needs, and significant improvement is needed for them to be successful in it. It is worth exploring whether the role, the team or the environment is the right fit for them. A performance conversation and a clear plan of action are required.

New in the job

A colleague who has been in their role for less than six months, so it is too soon to assess their performance.

Questions people ask

Why have the dates moved this year?

We have brought the process forward so that it is complete before the year end. That means we can go into 2027 with our focus on setting and delivering the objectives for the year ahead, and with ratings already in place for the annual pay review in January. The performance window opens on 19th October, manager ratings close on 13th November and calibration finishes on 27th November.

How do I rate a year that has not finished yet?

By this point in the year you should have a very good indication of how each colleague has performed, so rate on the evidence you have. A rating reflects the whole year, and one piece of work finishing in December rarely changes that picture.

What is calibration for?

Calibration brings managers together to compare ratings so that the same standard applies whichever team a colleague works in. It covers all colleagues, and its purpose is fairness and consistency.

Can a rating change after I have entered it?

Yes, it can. Ratings can move during calibration and remain provisional until the sign-off stages are complete, which is why they should not be shared as final before then.

Who can help me?

Your People Business Partner is your first point of contact for questions about the process, a difficult conversation or a rating you are unsure about.
